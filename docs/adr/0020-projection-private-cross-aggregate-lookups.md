# 0020. Projections Resolve Cross-Aggregate Identity Through Projection-Private Lookups

## Status

Accepted (May 2026)

## Context

Phase 6's projections compose state from events across multiple bounded contexts. `OrderListProjection` after this phase observes Sales (`OrderPlaced`, `OrderShipped`, `OrderCancelled`, `OrderCompleted`) plus Fulfillment (`ShipmentScheduled`, `ShipmentReturned`). `OrderDetailProjection` observes all sixteen Sales, Fulfillment, and Billing events the readiness doc names. Both projections key their read models on `OrderId`.

The divergence pre-flight surfaced at commit 3: `ShipmentReturned(ShipmentId, Reason, ReturnedUtc)` carries no `OrderId`. `ShipmentDispatched` and `ShipmentDelivered` are the same shape, carrying `ShipmentId` only. The Shipment aggregate's events leave Sales' `OrderId` off the payload after the initial `ShipmentScheduled` event, which carries both `ShipmentId` and `OrderId`. The shape is correct for the Shipment aggregate's own purposes (its events are about the shipment's lifecycle, not the order's) and follows the same lean-event discipline Phase 4 settled on for cross-context references: the original event carries the correlation, the follow-on events trust the correlation is already known.

The write side has a legitimate move for resolving the missing identifier. A process manager handling `ShipmentReturned` (the Return PM is the worked case at `src/ProcessManagers/Returns/ReturnProcessManagerHandler.cs:47`) loads the Shipment aggregate by `ShipmentId` and reads `OrderId` off the loaded state. The PM is on the write side; it has access to aggregate-load paths and uses them for orchestration. ADR 0014 codifies the PM dispatch path; the PM's aggregate-load access is a write-side capability not available to read-side consumers.

The read side cannot use that move. A projection that loads an aggregate to resolve identity reaches across the read-write seam in the wrong direction: it forces the read-side handler to depend on a write-side load, couples the projection's catch-up latency to the aggregate's hydration cost, and creates an undefined ordering between the load and the projection's checkpoint. The Phase 6 readiness doc names workflow-state inference from domain events, not PM streams, as the read-side rule, and CLAUDE.md keeps projections off the write side entirely; this ADR makes the rule load-bearing for cross-aggregate identity resolution specifically.

Phase 6 ships four projections that need this resolution: `OrderListProjection` for the return-state surface, `OrderDetailProjection` for both the three shipment-update events and the three OrderId-less payment events, plus the two reverse-lookup patterns already shipped (`CustomerSummaryProjection`'s `customer_summary_orders` for `OrderCancelled` netting, `InventoryDashboardProjection`'s `inventory_reservations` for `InventoryReleased` reversal). The same general shape resolves every case: a projection-private lookup table populated by the event carrying both identifiers, queried by the event carrying only one.

Two alternatives exist and both fail.

A shared cross-projection lookup (a fifth standalone projection, parallel to `SkuToInventoryIdProjection` and `OrderIdToPaymentIdProjection`) would advance its checkpoint independently of the consuming projection's checkpoint. Replay or rebuild can interleave the two checkpoints in any order, producing a window where the consuming projection observes the lookup-resolved event before the lookup projection has observed the population event. The window is rare in live tail but routine at rebuild. The cross-projection race forces either retry-with-backoff on the consuming side, coordinated rebuild ordering across projections, or accepting intermittent rebuild-test failures. None is acceptable.

A direct payload-widening (adding `OrderId` to `ShipmentReturned` and the other shipment-update events) is the move the cross-context vocabulary doc deliberately rejected. The lean-event discipline keeps each event's payload at the aggregate's own minimum, with cross-context references on the originating event and not duplicated thereafter. Widening every Shipment event to carry `OrderId` creates a drift surface (the duplicated `OrderId` could disagree with the `ShipmentScheduled` one across versions) and dissolves the Phase 4 design.

## Decision

Projections that consume events carrying only one of a needed identifier pair maintain their own private lookup table, populated by the event that carries both identifiers, queried by the event that carries only one. The lookup table lives in the `read_models` schema alongside the projection's primary read model, written under the same unit-of-work transaction, advanced by the same checkpoint.

Projections never resolve cross-aggregate identity by loading aggregates. The aggregate-load path (`IRepository<TAggregate>.LoadAsync`, the write-side path PMs legitimately use) is off-limits to projections. The forbidding is structural: projections live in `src/Projections/`, which has no `ProjectReference` to the write-side composition root, and the ports projections receive (`I{Name}Store`, `I{Name}UnitOfWork`, `ICheckpointStore`) carry no aggregate-load capability.

The rule applies to two distinct cases that share the same shape:

- **Cross-aggregate identity resolution.** The event the projection wants to act on carries one identifier; the projection needs the other to key its read model. Worked cases: `OrderListProjection` resolving `OrderId` from `ShipmentReturned`'s `ShipmentId` through `order_list_shipments`; `OrderDetailProjection` resolving `OrderId` from any of the three shipment-update events through `order_detail_shipments`, and from the three OrderId-less payment events (`PaymentCaptured`, `PaymentRefunded`, `PaymentVoided`) through `order_detail_payments`.
- **Reverse-of-magnitude resolution.** The compensating or terminal event carries correlation identity but not magnitude; the projection recovers magnitude from its own prior row. Worked cases: `CustomerSummaryProjection` recovering `(CustomerId, Total)` from `customer_summary_orders` on `OrderCancelled`; `InventoryDashboardProjection` recovering `Quantity` from `inventory_reservations` on `InventoryReleased`.

Both cases use the same mechanism: a projection-private table that the projection writes from the events that carry the full information and reads from the events that don't. The mechanism's correctness rests on `GlobalPosition` ordering: the event that populates the lookup row arrives before the event that queries it, because the events are causally ordered (a shipment is scheduled before it is returned, an order is placed before it is cancelled, a reservation exists before it is released). The checkpoint sequence makes the ordering enforceable: the lookup row exists in the same transactional state the query sees.

A not-found lookup is a no-op with a debug-level log entry, never a throw. Not-found is the correct behavior for events that arrive without their causal predecessor visible to the projection (a rebuild that starts from a truncated history, an event for an aggregate the projection never observed creation of, a race in test fixtures). The projection's job is to reflect what it has observed, not to enforce invariants on the upstream event stream.

## Consequences

- Each projection that subscribes to a cross-context follow-on event (one that carries less than the full identifier set) ships with a projection-private lookup table. The lookup table is part of the projection's migration, written under the same unit of work, advanced by the same checkpoint. The table is not exposed through a separate store port; it is an implementation detail of the projection's `I{Name}UnitOfWork`.
- Phase 6 ships five such tables: `customer_summary_orders` (ADR 0019), `inventory_reservations` (D10), `order_list_shipments` (this commit), and `order_detail_shipments` plus `order_detail_payments` (cluster 5; `order_detail_payments` resolves the three OrderId-less payment events, the same rule applied to the Billing side as the shipment events apply to Fulfillment). The naming convention is `{projection_table}_{predecessor}` where the predecessor names the upstream relation the lookup captures.
- The cross-context vocabulary doc gains a cross-reference to this ADR for the read-side identity-resolution rule, paralleling its existing access-pattern-2 statement of the write-side identifier-translation rule (a PM consults a projection-maintained lookup, never another context's aggregate state or another PM's stream).
- The rule does not displace the `SkuToInventoryIdProjection` and `OrderIdToPaymentIdProjection` standalone-lookup pattern Session 0009 shipped for the process managers. PMs reading those lookups have access to write-side coordination mechanisms (the idempotency store, the delay queue, the load-or-new repository pattern) that close the cross-checkpoint race the read side cannot close. The standalone-lookup pattern is appropriate for write-side consumers; the projection-private pattern is appropriate for read-side consumers. The two patterns coexist at different layers.
- Future projections that grow a cross-aggregate identity dependency follow the rule. The pre-flight check at the projection's first commit is whether the events the projection subscribes to carry the identifiers the read model keys on; if not, a projection-private lookup table joins the migration.
- The rule does not require every projection to use a lookup table. Projections whose events carry all needed identifiers (`SkuToInventoryIdProjection` keying on `Sku` from `InventoryCreated`, the existing `OrderListProjection` keying on `OrderId` from Sales-only events before this phase) ship without lookup tables. The rule applies when, and only when, the event payload set is insufficient.
- Track A flag against Chapter 13. The chapter's `ch13_crossContext_b` covers anti-corruption-layer translation at cross-context projection boundaries but does not depict the lookup-table pattern this ADR establishes. Phase 17 reconciliation extends the chapter's worked example to cover the pattern: either a new subsection on cross-aggregate identity resolution, or an extension of the cross-context-projections section showing the lookup-table mechanism. The flag rolls into the Phase 6 F-0010 block at session close.

## Trigger for revisiting

The projection-private lookup rule is reversible. Conditions that would justify reopening it:

- An event-payload widening lands in a future phase that puts the previously-missing identifier on every follow-on event (a Phase 15 versioning pass that adds `OrderId` to `ShipmentDispatched`, `ShipmentDelivered`, `ShipmentReturned`). The cross-context vocabulary's lean-event discipline would reverse; the projection-private lookup becomes redundant for that event family. The lookup table can be removed by migration at that point; the rule still applies to other event families that retain the lean shape.
- A read-side coordination mechanism emerges (a unified projection-checkpoint store with cross-projection ordering guarantees, a shared catch-up scheduler that advances multiple projections atomically) that closes the cross-projection race the standalone-lookup alternative currently fails. At that point the shared lookup becomes viable and the per-projection duplication becomes the higher cost. The trade-off would warrant re-evaluation.
- A future projection's lookup table grows to material size and the duplication across projections (two `_shipments` tables on different projections, two `_orders` tables) creates operational pain. The first move would be sharing the lookup as a separate read-side artifact while keeping the per-projection checkpoint coupling; the second would be reversing to a shared-projection model with explicit cross-projection ordering. Either move would warrant the revisit.

The trigger conditions are payload-driven or operational, not pedagogical. The pedagogical question (whether Chapter 13 should depict the lookup-table pattern) belongs to Phase 17 reconciliation.
